Quick Start Guide

Photograph the damage, say what you see, and send the whole job in one tap.

Collision Voice runs on your iPhone like any other app. For each vehicle you enter a few details, take photos of the damage, and talk to describe what you see — the app types your words for you. When you're done, one button sends everything as a single file you can open on your computer.

Set up (once)

  1. On your iPhone, open Chrome or Safari and go to gc-rnd.github.io/Collision-Voice
  2. Tap the Share button (square with an arrow) and choose Add to Home Screen. Now it opens like an app, full screen. (If Chrome doesn't show that choice, do this one step in Safari.)
  3. When asked, tap Allow for the camera, microphone and location. (Location is only used to stamp where each photo was taken.)
  4. The first time you dictate, the app downloads a small speech model, and the first time you scan a plate it downloads a plate reader. Do these once with a good connection. After that they work without internet.

Everything is saved on your phone as you go. If you close the app, lock the phone or lose signal, your job is still there when you come back. Nothing is cleared until you tap New job.

Doing an inspection

1Enter the job details

At the top of the screen, fill in the Assignment / Claim #, the Customer name and your name as Inspector. Choose the Inspection type (Initial, Supplement, Re-inspection, Final or Total loss).

The start time appears by itself as soon as you begin — you don't type it.

2Describe the vehicle

VIN: tap the small barcode icon in the VIN box and point the camera at the barcode (usually on the sticker inside the driver's door). It reads the barcode and fills in the VIN. Then the app looks up the year, make and model and fills those in too (this part needs internet).

License plate: tap the camera icon in the plate box, line the plate up inside the frame and hold steady for a moment. The plate is typed in for you.

Color: pick it from the list.

Always compare with the car. The scanners are very good but not perfect. If a scan won't work, tap Use a photo on the camera screen, or simply type it in.

3Add photos for each damage area

Each Area of Damage is one place on the vehicle.

  1. Choose the area from the list (for example Bumper Cover (Rear)).
  2. Tap 📷 Add photo and take your pictures. Take as many as you need — the photos go straight in with no extra questions.
  3. Tap ● Dictate overall note, say what you see, then tap Stop. After a moment your words appear as text. You can also just type.

Every photo gets a permanent image number (#1, #2, #3…) and is stamped with the date, time and location in its corner. A pencil next to a number means that photo already has a note.

Need another place on the car? Tap + Add observation to start the next damage area. To drop one you don't need, tap Remove.

4Add a note to each photo

Tap any small photo to open it full screen. One row of buttons sits right under the photo, and the note is below it:

  • ● Dictate — tap, describe this photo, tap Stop. The note is saved under that image number. Dictating again adds to the note. You can also type in the note box.
  • ‹ Prev and Next › — move through your photos without closing.
  • Done — close the photo and go back.
  • Delete — removes the photo. It asks "Are you sure?" first, because it can't be undone.

A fast way to work: take all your photos first. Then open the first photo, dictate, tap Next, dictate, and keep going down the line. Speak close to the phone and in a normal voice. Dictation understands English.

5Check your notes

Scroll to the bottom, to the Report card. It tells you how many photos you have, how big the job is and how many files it will be.

Tap View all notes to see everything in one place: the job details, the vehicle, and every image number with its note. This is a good last check before you send.

6Share the job

Tap Share. Your phone's share sheet opens, and you choose where the job goes:

  • OneDrive (or another cloud folder) — the job appears on your computer a moment later.
  • Mail — email it to yourself or someone else.
  • Save to Files — keep a copy on the phone.
  • AirDrop — send straight to a Mac nearby.

Everything — every photo (full size) and all the notes — goes as one file. A very large job is split into a few files; the Share button then says part 1 of 2, part 2 of 2… Tap Share once for each part.

The Upload button is greyed out. It's saved for a future feature.

Don't see OneDrive in the share sheet? Scroll the row of app icons at the top all the way to the right and tap More. Switch OneDrive on and tap the green + to put it in your Favorites so it stays on the list. Or choose Save to Files, then Browse → OneDrive.

7On your computer

Open the file from OneDrive (or your email). It is a zip file — right-click it and choose Extract All. Inside you'll find:

  • the photos, named IMG-001.jpg, IMG-002.jpg… The numbers match the image numbers in your notes.
  • notes.txt — all your notes with their image numbers. Open it and copy what you need into your estimate.
  • inspection.json and manifest.json — the same information in a form other software can read (you can ignore these).

The file name starts with the claim number, then the customer and the date and time, for example CLM-1042_Jane-Sample_2026-11-01_1259.zip, so one job never overwrites another.

8Start the next job

When the job is shared, tap New job at the bottom right. This clears the photos and notes from the phone to make room. If you haven't shared the job since your last change, the app warns you twice before it deletes anything.

Good to know

  • No signal? You can still take photos, dictate and type. Only the VIN lookup (year, make, model) needs internet, plus the first-time downloads mentioned above.
  • Photos are full size. The app never shrinks them. A job with many photos can be large, so use Wi-Fi when you share it.
  • Storage. The Report card shows how much room your jobs use. If it says storage is almost full, share the job and start a new one.
  • Please don't clear your browser's website data (in Chrome: Settings → Privacy → Delete Browsing Data; in Safari: Settings → Safari → Clear History and Website Data) while a job is unshared — that would delete it.
  • Updates are automatic. If the app looks out of date, tap the ☰ menu (top right) and choose Check for updates, or close the app completely and open it again. The same menu opens this guide and shows the version number — handy if you ever need to tell us which version you're using.

If something doesn't work

What you seeWhat to try
Camera or microphone won't startGo to your iPhone's Settings → Apps → Chrome (or Safari) and make sure Camera and Microphone are on. When the browser asks, tap Allow. Then reopen the app.
"Nothing heard — try again"Hold the phone closer, speak a little longer, and try again.
"Transcription failed"The very first time, the speech model needs internet to download. Connect and try again.
The VIN barcode won't scanImprove the light, hold steady, and fill the frame with the barcode. Tap Use a photo to take a still picture, or type the VIN. Not every VIN sticker has a barcode — the dashboard VIN plate usually doesn't.
Year, make and model didn't fill inYou need internet for the lookup, and the VIN must have all 17 characters. You can always pick them from the lists.
The plate was read wrongCheck it against the car. Try again with the plate flat on and well lit, or type it. The app may show a second guess you can tap.
The phone or computer asks for camera, microphone or location again and againThe app now asks for the microphone and your location only once per visit. When your browser asks, choose Allow while visiting the site (in Chrome) — not "Allow this time". Also check Settings → Apps → Chrome (or Safari) on the iPhone and make sure Camera, Microphone and Location are switched on. In Safari you can also tap aA → Website Settings and set them to Allow. On a computer, open the app from its web address, not from a saved file. A browser decides how long it remembers a choice, and the app can't change that.

Privacy

  • Your photos and notes stay on your phone until you choose to share them.
  • Speech-to-text and the VIN barcode and plate readers all run on the phone. No voice recording is kept — only the text.
  • The only thing that goes over the internet by itself is the VIN, sent to the US government's free NHTSA service to look up the year, make and model.
